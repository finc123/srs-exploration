Auto ABS Loss Curves

Cumulative losses as a % of each trust's original pool balance, by month since closing. Month 0 is closing (0%); month n is the nth monthly collection period. Source: the monthly servicer report (EX-99.1) in each trust's Form 10-D on SEC EDGAR. Covers trusts issued from 2020 onward that file publicly.

Implied recovery rate = recoveries ÷ gross charge-offs over a trailing 3- or 6-month window, where recoveries = gross charge-offs − net losses (both from the cumulative figures in each servicer report). Black lines pool every selected trust in a program (sum of recoveries ÷ sum of charge-offs). Windows start no earlier than month 3, since recoveries lag charge-offs, and need at least $0.5m of charge-offs.

Notes

  • Net: CarMax shows its reported "Ratio of Cumulative Net Losses to Initial Pool Balance". Carvana shows its reported "Aggregate Realized Loss percentage" (cumulative net charge-offs ÷ original pool balance as of the cutoff date).
  • Gross: cumulative charged-off principal before recoveries ÷ the same original pool balance. CarMax: cumulative "Defaulted Receivables (charge-offs)". Carvana: "Aggregate Gross Charged-Off Receivables losses".
  • Implied recovery rate: (gross charge-offs − net losses) ÷ gross charge-offs, from changes in the cumulative figures over the window. Definitions differ by issuer: CarMax nets "Recoveries" against defaulted principal; Carvana nets liquidation proceeds less liquidation expenses. Compare trends within an issuer rather than levels across issuers. Recoveries arrive after the charge-off (repossession, then auction), so a window with rising charge-offs reads low and one with falling charge-offs reads high; TR6M smooths this and the auction seasonality that shows in TR3M.
  • Carvana Non-prime (N) trusts after 2021 and some Prime trusts were sold as 144A deals and file no 10-D, so they cannot appear here.
  • When a filing is amended (10-D/A), the amended figure is used. A missing month is left as a gap; the curve is not shifted.
  • This is a snapshot (hand-published, not refreshed).
